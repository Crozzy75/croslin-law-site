Prenuptial Agreements

A prenup lets a couple decide, before they marry, how they will divide their property and handle spousal support if the marriage ends. We help you understand what is in a prenup, what best protects your interests, and how it may practically affect a future divorce.

Getting married soon? Start on your prenup now so you know your future is secure.

How we can help

  • Reviewing prenups drafted by your future spouse
  • Drafting prenups on your behalf
  • Negotiating prenups

What to expect

We start by understanding what you need going into prenup negotiations, whether you want to protect your assets or understand what your future spouse is asking for. Once we have a clear picture of your goals, we draft or review the prenup and go over its terms with you. We then negotiate with your future spouse and finalize everything before the big day.

Common questions

Can I address child custody or support in a prenup?

Oklahoma does not allow a prenup to decide what happens with child custody or child support. A court will always have the right to make those decisions, because both affect the best interests of your minor children.

Can I negotiate a postnup or modify an existing prenup after I marry?

Oklahoma law is unclear on whether a prenup can be modified after a couple is married, or whether spouses can enter into what is called a “postnuptial agreement.” Because of that, there is significant risk in negotiating a postnup or modifying an existing prenup, since either may be unenforceable. Talk with an attorney before changing an agreement you already have.

What normally happens to spousal support (alimony) in a prenup?

Prenups generally set the terms for any spousal support a spouse may receive if the couple divorces. They often require that the spouses be married a specific amount of time before any support is owed, and the amount may increase the longer the marriage lasts. Talk with an attorney to see what spousal support could look like for you in a prenup.

What happens to marital property if I have a prenup?

A prenup can set the rules for what counts as separate property and what counts as marital property. Many prenups provide that property acquired during the marriage remains separate unless the spouses agree in writing that it is marital. If you want to protect your separate property, call an attorney today.

Can I back out of a prenup I’ve already signed?

Oklahoma allows a prenup to be set aside if it was signed under fraud, duress, or coercion, or without full disclosure of assets, but it is not easy. To make sure that your prenup is enforceable, talk to an attorney today.
