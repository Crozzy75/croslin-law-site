Asset Protection

You’ve worked hard to build what you have. We’re here to help you keep it.

Worried about creditors? The sooner you plan, the more options you have.

How we can help

  • Reviewing how your property is owned
  • Planning to protect assets from future creditors
  • Setting up business entities
  • Assessing fraudulent transfer risks
  • Keeping your plan current

What to expect

We talk through what you own and what you want to accomplish, then go over your options and the trade-offs of each. We then implement your chosen strategy and help you keep it current.

Common questions

Can I protect my assets from creditors?

Often, yes. Some property (like retirement accounts) already has protection, and tools such as certain trusts and business entities can protect more. But protection usually means giving up some control, and it works best well before a problem arises. Transfers made to avoid an existing creditor can be undone. Talk with an attorney about the options that fit your situation.

Does forming an LLC protect my personal assets?

Yes, in two ways. An LLC can protect your personal property from the business’s debts and lawsuits, and it can protect the LLC’s assets from your personal creditors. That protection depends on forming the LLC properly and keeping your personal and business affairs separate. Asset transfers must be done correctly though, so call an attorney who can help you decide whether an LLC fits your situation.

Should I transfer assets into my spouse’s name?

No, not to shield them from creditors. Oklahoma law may allow a court to reverse a transfer like this. Giving property to your spouse can also mean losing it if your spouse has their own debts or files for divorce. Talk to an attorney about safer strategies to protect your assets.

Does an umbrella insurance policy replace asset protection?

No. An umbrella policy is a good way to reduce your exposure to some claims, but it doesn’t replace asset protection. Most policies have coverage limits, and if a claim goes beyond them, you could be personally responsible for the difference. Umbrella policies also cover some kinds of claims, but not all, meaning you would be less protected. Insurance and asset protection work best together, so talk with an attorney about how an umbrella policy fits into your plan.

Is a revocable or irrevocable trust right for me?

It depends on what you want the trust to do. A revocable trust lets you keep control and change it anytime, which makes it useful for avoiding probate, but it generally doesn’t protect your assets from your own creditors. An irrevocable trust is harder to change because you give up control, and that trade-off is usually what can make protection possible. Talk with an attorney about which fits your assets and your risks.
